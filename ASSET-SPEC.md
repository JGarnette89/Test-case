# Right of Way — Art Asset Specification (v1)

A brief for producing top-down art for *Right of Way*, a driving-examiner
game: you sit beside a candidate driver on a set course through a small
North American town, give the directions, and mark the faults you catch.
The view is straight down on the road, like a map that moves. The game
is a phone game first.

This document is self-contained. Everything you need is here; you do not
need to see the game or its code. Every dimension below is the real
dimension the game's own geometry uses, in metres, so an asset made to
these numbers will sit on the road at the right size with no adjustment.

---

## 0. Read this first: what the game can display today

The game draws everything with **vector shapes (SVG) generated by code**.
Roads are wide lines stroked along a path — which is what lets them bend
— with the painted markings drawn on top as more lines. Cars are rounded
rectangles with wheel, windshield and lamp shapes. Stop signs are drawn
octagons. Scenery is coloured rectangles and circles. **No image file is
loaded anywhere in the game today**, and the game's logic knows nothing
about colour or pictures; the drawing layer decides how things look.

So, plainly: **no art asset, in any format, drops in with zero code
changes.** What is true is that the change per kind of object is small
and mechanical *if the asset follows the conventions in this document*:
where a rounded rectangle is drawn for a car today, an image is placed
instead at the same anchor, the same size in metres and the same
rotation. That is one edit per object class, not a new renderer.

What that means for a **GTA 1 / GTA 2 top-down pixel look**, which is the
direction being considered:

1. **Sprites for objects are viable** — cars, people, signs, roadside
   props, buildings, trees. One top-down sprite per object. The renderer
   rotates images to any angle itself and can be told to keep pixels
   crisp (no smoothing), so a single sprite per object is enough. This is
   how GTA 2 worked: one sprite, rotated freely.
2. **Do not make rotation sets** (8, 16 or 32 pre-rendered angles). They
   would need code to pick frames and are not needed. If the source of an
   asset is a voxel or 3D model, good — it keeps lighting and proportions
   consistent across the set — but deliver a baked top-down PNG, not a
   model file.
3. **Road surfaces cannot be tiles.** Roads are continuous, they bend, and
   they are stroked along a path, so a "road tile" with markings baked in
   has no way to be laid. The only road-surface art the game can use is a
   **seamless, direction-free texture** (asphalt grain, concrete, grass)
   applied as a fill. The painted markings — centre line, edge lines,
   lane dividers, stop lines, crosswalk bars — **stay drawn by code** and
   must not be baked into any texture.
4. **Intersections are also drawn, not tiled**: the box is a square of
   asphalt where two roads cross, with the legs closed by a curb line
   where a leg does not exist (a T-intersection). No intersection tiles.
5. **Everything is strictly top-down orthographic.** No perspective, no
   isometric, no vanishing point. The camera looks straight down.

A second reality to design for: **the view shows a lot of road, so
everything is small on screen.** At native scale a car is 90 × 36 pixels,
but the game frames the road as a *duration ahead* rather than a
distance, and on a phone a car appears anywhere from about **8 px long**
(wide view, ten seconds of road ahead at 60 km/h) to about **45 px** (the
closest view, 36 m across the screen). Detail finer than about two screen pixels
does not read. **Silhouette and colour carry the asset; fine detail is
wasted.** The five car colours have to be unmistakable at 8 px.

## 1. Scale, origin and orientation

These four conventions decide whether an asset drops in or needs
fiddling. Follow them exactly.

**Metres, and 20 pixels per metre.** The game measures the world in
metres and its native drawing scale is **20 px per metre** (1 px =
5 cm). Author every raster asset at 20 px/m. If you also produce a
double-resolution version for sharper phones, put it in a separate `@2x`
folder at 40 px/m with identical framing; never mix scales in one folder.
For pixel art, work at exactly 20 px/m and do not resample.

**Anchor = the object's geometric centre.** The game positions every
object by the centre of its footprint (a car by the centre of its 4.5 ×
1.8 m rectangle, a pedestrian by the centre of their body, a sign by the
centre of its symbol). Make the image canvas exactly the footprint plus a
uniform **4 px margin** on every side (0.2 m — room for mirrors, a lamp
glow, an outline), and keep the footprint centred in the canvas, so
canvas centre = footprint centre. State the footprint in the manifest
(section 6), not by trimming the image.

**Forward is +x (to the right) in the unrotated sprite.** Heading 0°
points east/right on screen; angles increase clockwise (90° points
down/south, because screen y grows downward). Draw every vehicle and
pedestrian **horizontal, facing right**. The renderer applies the
rotation.

**Straight down, no perspective, no tilt.** A car sprite is its roof and
hood; the sides are at most a one-pixel hint of darker body colour. A
tree is a canopy disc. A building is a roof. Nothing leans.

**Lighting: from directly above, soft.** Because a single sprite rotates
with its object, any strong directional light baked into it will rotate
too and look wrong half the time. Shade by form only (a slightly lighter
centre of a roof, a darker edge), keep the overall light overhead and
diffuse, and **bake no cast shadow** — the renderer adds a small
drop-shadow of its own under vehicles and people. A one-pixel contact
shadow directly under the footprint is acceptable and should be the same
on every asset if used.

## 2. The road, and what is code-drawn (for reference, not for art)

Included so textures and props are made to fit, and so nobody paints
markings that the game will paint again. All in metres.

| element | dimension | notes |
|---|---|---|
| traffic lane | **3.6 m** wide | every road is one lane each way except the arterial (three each way) |
| road width, one lane each way | 7.2 m | residential and collector streets |
| road width, arterial | 21.6 m | three lanes each way, no parking |
| parking lane | **2.4 m**, outside the traffic lane | residential and collector only |
| centre line | yellow, 0.15 m wide, **3 m dash / 6 m gap** | code-drawn |
| edge line | white, 0.15 m wide, solid | code-drawn |
| lane divider (arterial) | white, 0.12 m wide, 3 m dash / 4.5 m gap | code-drawn |
| intersection box | (lanes × 3.6 m) square: **7.2 × 7.2 m** on ordinary streets, 21.6 m on an arterial | asphalt, square corners, no curb radius |
| stop line | white, **0.4 m** thick, across the inbound half of the road (3.6 m long on an ordinary street) | painted 5.65 m from the intersection centre, i.e. **2.05 m outside the box edge** |

## 3. Every object that needs art

Grouped by what the game does with it. For each: real dimensions (the
footprint the game positions and collides by), how many variants are
worth having, and rotation needs. **Rotation: "free" means the renderer
rotates a single right-facing sprite; never pre-render angles.**

### 3.1 Surfaces (seamless textures)

Used as fills. Must tile seamlessly in both directions with **no
directional features** (no baked lines, no arrows, no lighting gradient).
Deliver at 20 px/m as **128 × 128 px** (a 6.4 m tile); 256 × 256 is fine
for grain that would otherwise repeat visibly.

| texture | variants | notes |
|---|---|---|
| asphalt | 2: fresh, worn | base tone around #43474F; the markings are painted over it by code |
| driveway / apron concrete | 1 | a driveway apron is 4.85 m wide and runs 6 m back from the curb; drawn as a rectangle today in #3A3F47 |
| sidewalk concrete | 1 | the strip behind the curb |
| grass | 2: lawn, rough | ground in suburban (#5E8A54) and rural (#6C8F4C) settings |
| city ground / paving | 1 | ground in the city setting (#6A6D75) |
| gravel / dirt | 1 | rural shoulders and pads |
| roundabout island | 1 | grass or planting, 14 m disc — may be one round image rather than a tile |

### 3.2 Vehicles

All moving traffic is **one size: 4.5 m long × 1.8 m wide (90 × 36 px)**.
The physics and the collision test use that exact rectangle, so every
car body, whatever its shape, must fit inside it and fill most of it.
There are no trucks or buses in the game yet.

**Colour keys.** The game assigns each car one of five colour keys, and
draws it in that colour: **red, green, amber, blue, pale** (off-white).
Deliver every body style in all five. The candidate — the car being
examined — is drawn **amber** in the current build and is additionally
marked by the renderer with a blue ring, so no special candidate sprite
is needed; a distinct "learner" body (e.g. a plain sedan with a roof
sign) is welcome as a sixth option but not required.

**Body styles**: 4 is plenty — sedan, hatchback, wagon/SUV, pickup. Each
in 5 colours = 20 sprites.

**Lamp positions** (so the lights the code draws land on your lamp
housings). Measured from the car's centre, x forward, y to the driver's
right:

- turn signals: at all four corners, centred **0.22 m in from each end**
  and **0.24 m in from each side** (front-right ≈ (+2.03, +0.66)); draw
  the housings unlit — the renderer draws the lit lamp and its glow.
- brake lights: rear corners, a strip 0.22 m long starting 0.08 m from
  the rear, from 0.18 m to 0.56 m in from each side; unlit in the sprite.
- windshield: roughly from 0.5 m to 1.1 m ahead of centre, inset 0.22 m
  from the sides at the base; a cabin block about 2.4 m long over the
  middle of the car.

**Emergency vehicle**: the same 4.5 × 1.8 footprint, white body with a
battenburg or stripe, and a **roof lightbar 1.1 m long × 1.7 m wide**
(spanning nearly the roof) drawn UNLIT — the red/blue flashing is drawn
by code over it. 1 variant.

**Van (parked, blocks the view)**: **5.6 m × 2.3 m** (112 × 46 px).
Parked only; never drives. 2 colours (white, dark).

**Parked cars**: **4.6 m × 1.8 m** footprint; the driving-car sprites are
reused, so no separate set — but see 3.4 for where they sit.

Rotation: free. Wheels: static; no animation.

### 3.3 Pedestrians

Collision footprint is a **0.5 m radius** circle (1.0 m across). Drawn
today as an ellipse **0.44 × 0.68 m** (shoulders) with a head **0.34 m**
across. Canvas **28 × 28 px** (1.0 m footprint + margin), facing right.

Variants: 6–8 — adult A/B, child, person with bags, person with a
stroller, person on a phone (looks down; this one is a hazard type the
game wants to read at a glance), elderly with a cane. Colours: mixed,
muted, must not clash with the five car colours (do not use the car red,
green, amber or blue as a coat colour).

Someone **waiting at a crossing button** is shown by the code drawing a
lit push-button box (0.42 × 0.62 m) beside them and a ring around them;
no separate sprite.

No cyclists (not in the game). Rotation: free. Static pose; a 2-frame
walk is optional and NOT wanted yet.

### 3.4 Curbside props — what stands along a street

The game lays a strip along each side of a street with **one slot every
6 m**, fills a share of the slots according to the street type, and
leaves the rest as driveways. All props are positioned by their centre,
rotated to the road's heading (so make them **horizontal, long axis
along +x**, as if the road ran left–right beneath them).

Where things sit, measured from the road's centreline on a one-lane-each-
way street (add 3.6 m per extra lane on an arterial):

| | distance from centreline |
|---|---|
| edge of the traffic lane | 3.6 m |
| centre of a parked car (in the 2.4 m parking lane) | 4.8 m |
| the curb | 6.0 m (10.8 m on the arterial: three lanes and no parking lane) |
| centre of a prop on the verge/sidewalk, and where people stand | 7.2 m (curb + 1.2 m) |

Nothing is parked within **9 m** of an intersection.

| prop | footprint (length × width) | canvas at 20 px/m | variants | street types |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| parked car | 4.6 × 1.8 m | reuse vehicle sprites | — | residential, collector |
| hedge | 6.0 × 1.2 m | 128 × 32 | 2 (trimmed, overgrown) | residential |
| garden wall / fence | 8.0 × 0.6 m | 168 × 20 | 2 (brick, wood fence) | residential |
| bins | 1.0 × 1.0 m | 28 × 28 | 2 (one bin, two bins) | residential |
| street furniture | 0.8 × 0.8 m | 24 × 24 | 4 (bench, planter, post box, lamp post base) | collector, arterial |
| shopfront strip | 7.0 × 1.0 m | 148 × 28 | 2 (awning, plain) | collector |
| bus shelter | 3.6 × 1.6 m | 80 × 40 | 1 | arterial |
| sandwich-board signage | 0.6 × 0.6 m | 20 × 20 | 2 | arterial |
| driveway apron | 4.85 m wide, 6.0 m deep from the curb, plus the 2.4 m parking lane it crosses | texture, 3.1 | 1 | residential |

**Street types** the game uses today, which decide what appears where:

- **residential**: 30 km/h, one lane each way, stop-controlled, parallel
  parking, driveways; roadside is 85% filled with parked cars, hedges,
  walls and bins; the most people about.
- **collector**: ~40 km/h, one lane each way, stop-controlled, parallel
  parking, no driveways; roadside 50% filled with parked cars, street
  furniture and shopfronts.
- **arterial**: 50 km/h, three lanes each way, no stop control, no
  parking, no driveways; roadside 20% filled with furniture, shelters and
  signage.

### 3.5 Signs and signals

Signs are drawn as **oversized map symbols, 1.4 m across**, because a
real 0.75 m sign face would be a dot at this scale. Keep that convention:
a sign is its face, seen flat from above, 1.4 m across (28 px canvas +
margin), centred on the post position. Do not draw the post. Rotation:
none needed (a symbol reads the same way up regardless).

| sign | variants | status |
|---|---|---|
| STOP (red octagon, white border, "STOP") | 1 | in use everywhere |
| YIELD | 1 | not in the game yet; cheap, make it |
| speed limit 30 / 40 / 50 km/h | 3 | not in the game yet; cheap, make them |
| NO PARKING | 1 | not in the game yet; optional |
| traffic signal head (0.8 × 1.7 m, three lamps) | 1, unlit | signals are not live content yet — **low priority** |
| crossing push-button box (0.42 × 0.62 m) | — | drawn by code; skip |

### 3.6 Buildings and scenery (off-road)

Three settings exist: **city**, **suburban**, **rural**. Today the
scenery is abstract blocks and discs, so this is the group with the most
freedom — but it is also the group that shows least, because the camera
follows the road. Roofs only. Footprints in whole metres, anchored at
the centre, no rotation needed beyond 0°/90° (make them axis-aligned).

| kind | footprints | variants | settings |
|---|---|---|---|
| house roof | 8 × 8, 10 × 8, 12 × 10 m | 4 | suburban, rural |
| commercial block roof | 16 × 12, 24 × 16, 30 × 20 m | 3 | city, collector frontages |
| tower block roof | 20 × 20, 30 × 30 m | 2 | city |
| tree canopy | 4, 6, 8 m discs | 3 per setting (deciduous, conifer, bare) | all |
| parking lot / pad | texture, 3.1 | — | city, suburban |
| lawn / field patch | texture, 3.1 | — | suburban, rural |

Roof art should be quiet: low contrast, a single roof material, an
inset edge, nothing that competes with the road.

## 4. Format, naming and file layout

**Format**: PNG, RGBA (transparent background), sRGB, no colour profile
tricks, no premultiplied halos. Pixel art: nearest-neighbour only, no
anti-aliasing on the outline, no partial-alpha edge pixels except where
deliberate. Native scale 20 px/m; optional `@2x` at 40 px/m with the same
framing.

**One object per file.** No sprite sheets — individual images are what
the renderer places. (If a sheet is unavoidable, include a JSON atlas
mapping each name to `x, y, w, h`; but prefer single files.)

**Naming**: lower-case, hyphenated, no spaces:

```
<group>-<object>-<variant>[-<colour>].png
```

- groups: `veh`, `ped`, `prop`, `sign`, `tex`, `bldg`
- variant: a letter, `a` `b` `c` …
- colour (vehicles only): `red` `green` `amber` `blue` `pale`

Examples: `veh-sedan-a-red.png`, `veh-sedan-a-amber.png`,
`veh-pickup-b-pale.png`, `veh-emergency-a.png`, `veh-van-a-white.png`,
`ped-adult-a.png`, `ped-phone-a.png`, `prop-hedge-b.png`,
`prop-shelter-a.png`, `sign-stop.png`, `sign-speed-40.png`,
`tex-asphalt-a.png`, `bldg-house-10x8-c.png`, `bldg-tree-6-conifer.png`.

**Folder layout** (this is where they will live in the project):

```
public/art/
  manifest.json
  vehicles/     veh-*.png
  pedestrians/  ped-*.png
  props/        prop-*.png
  signs/        sign-*.png
  textures/     tex-*.png
  buildings/    bldg-*.png
  @2x/          same tree, 40 px/m
```

**Manifest**: one `manifest.json` listing every file with the facts the
renderer needs, so integration is mechanical:

```json
{
  "scale": 20,
  "assets": [
    { "file": "vehicles/veh-sedan-a-red.png", "group": "veh",
      "footprint": [4.5, 1.8], "canvas": [98, 44], "anchor": "center",
      "forward": "+x", "colour": "red", "variant": "a" },
    { "file": "props/prop-hedge-a.png", "group": "prop",
      "footprint": [6.0, 1.2], "canvas": [128, 32], "anchor": "center",
      "forward": "+x", "variant": "a" },
    { "file": "textures/tex-asphalt-a.png", "group": "tex",
      "tile": [6.4, 6.4], "canvas": [128, 128], "seamless": true }
  ]
}
```

`footprint` is in metres, `canvas` in pixels (footprint × 20 + 8 for the
margin). If a footprint in the manifest disagrees with this document,
this document wins.

## 5. Style constraints that keep the set coherent

Consistency across the set matters more than any single asset. These are
the rules that make twenty cars and forty props look like one game.

**Palette.** Start from the game's existing colours and stay in their
family:

| role | hex |
|---|---|
| asphalt | #43474F |
| painted line white | #FAFAF2 |
| marking yellow | #FFC93C |
| outline / ink | #191C22 |
| car red | #E05252 |
| car green | #3BAA51 |
| car blue | #3B7BE8 |
| car amber (the candidate) | #F0A93C |
| car pale | #F2E8D5 |
| lamp / signal amber | #FFB330 |
| emergency red / blue | #E0473F / #2F6BE0 |
| grass | #5E8A54, darker #4E7746 |
| city ground | #6A6D75 |
| suburban ground | #5E8A54 |
| rural ground | #6C8F4C |
| driveway concrete | #3A3F47 |
| parked car (neutral) | #5A616B |
| hedge | #3D5A3A |
| wall | #4A4A46 |
| shopfront | #5C5148 |
| page background | #16181C |

Cars use their key colour for the body; everything that is not a car
avoids those five hues so a car always reads as a car. Keep saturation
moderate — the road is dark grey and the four car colours already carry
the contrast.

**Outlines.** Every object gets a **1 px dark outline (#191C22)** at 20
px/m, the same weight on everything. This is what keeps an 8 px car
legible against asphalt.

**Lighting.** Overhead, diffuse, no cast shadows (section 1). The same
on every asset.

**Top-down means top-down.** Roof view, no sides, no perspective, no
isometric cheat. If a prop has height (a shelter, a tree), show it as
its plan view with at most a one-pixel hint of a side.

**Readability at 8 px.** Check every vehicle and pedestrian at one-fifth
size. If the colour and the silhouette do not survive, the sprite has
too much going on.

**Pixel discipline (if pixel art).** One pixel grid, no sub-pixel
positioning, no rotated pixels in the source, no mixed resolutions.
Rotation happens in the game, not in the file.

## 6. What NOT to make yet

Effort spent on these has nowhere to go today:

- **Rotation sets** or angle-baked sprite sheets (the renderer rotates).
- **Road tiles, intersection tiles, corner pieces, or any surface with
  markings baked in** — roads and markings are drawn by code and bend.
- **Isometric, 3/4 or perspective** art of any kind.
- **Traffic signals in a lit state**, or signal timing art — signals are
  not live content; the unlit head in 3.5 is enough.
- **Cyclists, trucks, buses, motorcycles** — no such road users exist.
- **Animation**: wheel rotation, walk cycles, blinking lights, brake
  lights — the renderer draws lamp states itself; sprites are static.
- **Damage, collision or crash art** — contact is not yet a state the
  game responds to.
- **Night, rain, snow, seasons** — one daylight set only.
- **Interface art** — buttons, panels, icons, the marking sheet — the
  screens are styled in code and use an icon font.
- **Roundabout art beyond the island texture** — roundabouts exist but
  are a small part of play.
- **Anything larger than the canvas rule allows** — a 4.5 m car in a
  98 × 44 canvas; a car drawn with a 20 px margin will sit off its lane.

## 7. A minimum useful first delivery

If the set is produced in stages, this order gives the game something
usable earliest:

1. `tex-asphalt-a`, `tex-grass-a`, `tex-concrete-sidewalk-a` — three
   textures.
2. `veh-sedan-a` in all five colours, and `veh-emergency-a`.
3. `ped-adult-a`, `ped-adult-b`, `ped-child-a`.
4. `sign-stop`.
5. `prop-parked` is covered by the sedan; then `prop-hedge-a`,
   `prop-wall-a`, `prop-bins-a`.
6. Everything else.
